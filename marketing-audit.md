Comerz marketing page UI audit
Reading this as: a public SaaS landing page for Argentine shop owners, with a more expressive, editorial brand wrapped around a deliberately utilitarian product.
The page is usable and its copy often sounds like a real Argentine shop conversation. The template-like feeling comes from its visual system: nearly every idea is presented as centered text, a pale rounded card, a pill, or a framed dashboard screenshot. Motion adds polish, but rarely changes how the story is told. This is an assessment of the page’s appearance, not a claim about how it was made.
I inspected the full landing implementation, globals.css, image assets, and the running page at desktop and phone sizes. No code was changed. The local preview could not download Geist or Geist Mono and used fallback fonts, so the rendered typography observations have that limit.
1. Visual hierarchy
- The hero has many competing entry points. The “Nuevo” pill, large headline, long paragraph, two buttons, three reassurance notes, and dashboard frame all use familiar SaaS emphasis. The product image begins below the first desktop viewport and is barely introduced in the first phone viewport. Three visual directions
These are visual treatments of the current page, without proposing a new section order or information architecture.
Direction	Visual language	Product treatment	Motion
1. The shop in motion	Warm, confident, human. Bold sans typography, Comerz orange as the dominant accent, photography of real Argentine shops and owners. More physical texture and fewer floating cards.	Show the POS in context at a counter, then use tightly cropped real UI details where a feature is being explained.	Motion follows shop actions: a sale, stock decrement, end-of-day check. Mostly purposeful transitions.
2. Merchant’s field guide	Editorial and systematic. An asymmetrical grid, large numbered statements, strong captions, generous whitespace, and small receipt or inventory-label cues. Warm off-white, near-black, and a disciplined orange accent.	Treat screenshots as annotated evidence: one clear crop, one takeaway, one precise callout. Keep the product’s Geist-based UI intact inside those images.	Restrained reveals and deliberate chapter transitions; typography and composition provide the energy.
3. Commercial signal	High-contrast, graphic, and memorable. Oversized compact headlines, strong blocks of color, sharp alignment changes, and storefront-signage cues derived from the logo.	Alternate large authentic product crops with simple graphic explanations; avoid fake browser chrome and dashboard mosaics.	More kinetic type and directional movement, used sparingly around major claims.


My strongest recommendation is Merchant’s field guide. It creates the clearest distinction between expressive marketing and utilitarian product UI while keeping Comerz credible to a shop owner. Takenos shows the value of placing a product in human context; Lemon and Revex show stronger typographic scale and compositional confidence. Commercy is useful for the specificity of its commercial messaging, though Comerz should retain its own voice and visual identity.
